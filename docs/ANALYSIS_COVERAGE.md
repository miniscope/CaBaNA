# Analysis coverage

Survey of standardized neural-behavioral analyses compiled by Samara (lab-internal, 2026) from 100 papers (see REFERENCES.md), with CaBaNA's implementation status.

## Analyses as pipeline stages

| | Prepare | Group frames into | Statistic | Null | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PETH | z-score or ΔF/F; keep bouts above a minimum length and gap | windows around event onsets | mean over events of a(t_k+τ) minus the baseline mean | none | cells by time matrix |
| auROC | any trace type; frame mask | behavior on and behavior off | P(a_on > a_off) from the rank sum | circular shift, 1,000 times | one value and a class per cell |
| Rate map and spatial information | speed above cutoff; smoothing | spatial bins, with occupancy | λ = smoothed events / smoothed occupancy; SI = Σ p_b (λ_b/λ̄) log2(λ_b/λ̄) | circular shift, 1,000 times | one value and a flag per cell, plus a map |
| Stability across days | as for rate maps, on two sessions, plus CellReg matches | same spatial bins | Pearson r between a cell's two maps; population-vector correlation per bin | pair the cell with other cells | one r per cell; bin by bin matrix |
| PCA and population vectors | z-score per cell | time bins or conditions | SVD of the centered matrix; distance between condition means | shuffle condition labels | scores, loadings, variance explained |

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Event-aligned averages (PETH) | Neural: frames × cells, z-scored ΔF/F.<br>Behavior: event onset times (s) from a bout table | Keep bouts ≥ 2 s that start ≥ 5 s after the previous bout. Window −5 to +5 s (range ±2 to ±15). Subtract or z-score from a pre-event baseline (e.g. −5 to −2 s). Optional 100 ms–1 s bins. Smooth deconvolved events before averaging. | Average the aligned windows across events for each cell. | Descriptive. Pre vs post window compared across animals. | Mean trace, peak, latency. Heatmap of cells × time. | Timing and sign of responses around onset. | Kingsbury 2019; Wu 2021; Sun 2026; Zhao 2026 |
| 2. Mean activity per state (rate codes) | Neural: frames × cells, ΔF/F or deconvolved.<br>Behavior: a state label for every frame. | Minimum bout length; optionally drop transition frames. Smooth deconvolved events. | Mean activity in state A and state B, and their difference. | Difference vs circular-shift null (not a frame-wise t-test). | Means, difference, p, label per cell. | Fraction of cells preferring each state. | Liu 2025; Figge-Schlensok 2025; Reis 2021 (eLife); Shuman 2020 |
| 3. auROC selectivity | Neural: one trace per cell, z-scored ΔF/F.<br>Behavior: binary vector, 1 = behavior on. | Exclude frames of other behaviors or a buffer around bouts when comparing against baseline. Set a minimum circular shift (several seconds or more). | Area under the ROC curve: P(activity during behavior > activity outside it). | 1,000 circular shifts; excited > 97.5th, inhibited < 2.5th percentile. | auROC, null percentile, label per cell. | Which cells encode which behavior. | Kingsbury 2019; Wu 2021; Raam 2026; Liu 2025 (bin shuffle) |
| 4. Overlap of cell populations vs chance | Two cell classifications on the same cells (e.g. from auROC), or cells matched across days. | Thresholds come from the upstream classification. For cross-day overlap, a registration confidence cutoff (CellReg P_same). | Observed overlap k; expected = nA × nB / N. | Hypergeometric or Fisher exact test. | k, expected, odds ratio, p. | Shared vs dedicated populations. | Zhang 2024 (Nature); Duarte 2024; Zaki 2024; Sun 2026 |
| 5. Tuning to continuous variables | Neural: frames × cells, ΔF/F or z-scored.<br>Behavior: continuous variable per frame (speed, distance, angle). | Smooth position before computing speed. Choose bins for the variable and a minimum occupancy per bin. Restrict to moving frames where relevant. Consider a lag. | Mean activity per bin divided by occupancy; correlation with the variable. | Correlation or tuning strength vs circular shifts. | Correlation, tuning curve, preferred value. | Speed or distance coding; checks for movement confounds. | Ajabi 2023; Skromne Carrasco 2026; Kingsbury 2020; Schuette 2020 |
| 6. Rate maps and spatial information | Neural: frames × cells, deconvolved or binarized events.<br>Behavior: x, y in cm and speed. | Speed > 2–5 cm/s (range 1–20). Bins 2–5 cm (range 1–8). Gaussian smoothing σ 2–6 cm on activity and occupancy. Drop bins with < 100 ms occupancy. Minimum number of events per cell. | Skaggs spatial information from the occupancy-normalized rate map. | Information vs 500–1,000 circular shifts, > 95th percentile; often also a half-session stability criterion. | Rate map, information, place-cell flag, field size and centroid. | Place-cell fraction and field properties. | Zong 2022; Shuman 2020; Sun 2024; Etter 2020 |
| 7. Stability and remapping | Rate maps or tuning curves from two session halves or two days, same cells. | Same filters and smoothing as the maps being compared. Registration confidence cutoff. Decide whether to include cells inactive in one session. | Rate-map correlation per cell; population-vector correlation per spatial bin. | Correlation vs shuffled cell pairings. | Correlation per cell, PV correlation matrix. | Stable vs drifting or remapped codes. | Sun 2019; Schuette 2020; Keinath 2022; Blair 2023 |
| 8. PCA and population vectors | Neural: time bins × cells.<br>Behavior: a condition or time label per bin or trial. | Bin activity (100 ms–1 s). Z-score each cell. Average across trials, or keep single trials. Optional Gaussian smoothing of trajectories. | Singular value decomposition; distances between condition means over time. | Distance vs label shuffle (PCA alone is descriptive). | Trajectories, variance explained, distance curve. | Whether and when the population separates conditions. | Kingsbury 2019; Wu 2021; Keinath 2022; Sun 2026 |
| 9. Population decoding (linear SVM) | Neural: samples × cells (frames, bins or bouts).<br>Behavior: a label per sample. | Bin size. Z-score using the training fold only. Balance classes by subsampling. Hold out whole bouts or blocks of time. Subsample to a common number of cells across animals. | Linear SVM, cross-validated. | Accuracy vs shuffled labels or shifted activity, tested across animals. | Accuracy per animal; accuracy vs number of cells and vs time. | How much information the population carries and when it appears. | Klaus 2017; Wu 2021; Raam 2026; Sun 2026 |
| 10. GLM encoding model | Neural: time bins × one cell.<br>Behavior: design matrix of states, speed, position. | Bins of 100–200 ms. Expand behavior with temporal basis functions. Standardize continuous regressors. Ridge penalty chosen by cross-validation on blocks of time. | Fit activity as a weighted sum of regressors (Gaussian or Poisson). | Drop in held-out fit when a regressor is removed, vs shift null. | Weights, response kernels, unique contribution per variable. | Encoding with correlated behaviors controlled for. | Kingsbury 2019; Reis 2021 (J Neurosci); Sun 2022; Suto 2026 |

## Notes for CaBaNA

- **Trace type depends on the analysis.** Event-aligned and population-level analyses (PETH, pre/post tests, auROC, overlap, correlation, PCA, PV distances, linear-SVM decoding) mostly use z-scored ΔF/F (e.g. 27 of 53 PETH papers, 16 of 23 PCA papers, 9 of 18 auROC papers) or plain ΔF/F. Spatial and tuning analyses (rate maps, place-field stability, Bayesian decoding, tuning curves) mostly use deconvolved or binarized events (e.g. 16 deconvolved and 14 binarized of 28 rate-map papers; 12 and 10 of 19 stability papers). Metrics should therefore accept any trace type, with the trace choice recorded.
- **Null distributions recur across a small set of designs.** Circular (time) shift is the default for auROC, mean-per-state, tuning, correlation and rate-map information; label shuffle is used for decoding, PV distances and PCA separation; cell-identity shuffle is used for population overlap (alongside hypergeometric/Fisher tests); a bin/bout-level shuffle appears for auROC in one case (Liu 2025, written "bin shuffle" in the survey); a shuffled-cell pairing is the null for place-field and PV stability. A shared shuffle engine that wraps any metric covers most of these.
- **Frame-wise tests are discouraged.** Frames are autocorrelated, so Samara's notes prefer circular-shift nulls over frame-wise t-tests for state comparisons, and require held-out folds (whole bouts or blocks of time) for decoding and GLMs.
- **Most rows end with a test across animals.** Pre/post, decoding, PETH and similar analyses report significance as paired tests or per-animal accuracy across animals, not just per cell, so results need to be organized per animal and aggregated across animals.
